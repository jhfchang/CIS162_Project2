Summer Constellation Spotlight

Summer nights in Southern California bring Scorpius, Sagittarius, and Cygnus into view low in the south and overhead, each easy to spot once the key stars are identified. Perseid meteor shower also lights up the sky in mid-August, it is one of the most active showers of the year.

Scorpius

Shaped like a curved scorpion's tail, Scorpius is anchored by the bright red supergiant Antares. It sits low in the south during summer evenings, best seen away from tall obstructions on the horizon.

Click here for a real night-sky photo of this constellation.

  • Main Star: Antares
  • Viewing Direction: Low, South
  • Best Viewing Time: Summer evenings, 9PM - 11PM

Sagittarius

Best known for the Teapot asterism, Sagittarius points toward the center of the Milky Way. It rides low in the south just east of Scorpius on summer nights.

Click here for a real night-sky photo of this constellation.

  • Main Star: Kaus Australis
  • Viewing Direction: Low, South, east of Scorpius
  • Best Viewing Time: Summer evenings, 9PM - 11PM

Cygnus

Also called the Northern Cross, Cygnus is marked by the bright star Deneb at its tail. It flies high overhead through the summer Milky Way, wings stretched out.

Click here for a real night-sky photo of this constellation.

  • Main Star: Deneb
  • Viewing Direction: High overhead
  • Best Viewing Time: Summer evenings, 9PM - 1AM

Perseids Meteor Shower

One of the most active showers of the year, the Perseids peak around mid-August and can produce up to 60 meteors per hour under dark skies. They radiate from the constellation Perseus, low in the northeast after midnight.

  • Radiant: Constellation Perseus
  • Viewing Direction: Northeast, after midnight
  • Best Viewing Time: Peaks August 12–13 (2026)
