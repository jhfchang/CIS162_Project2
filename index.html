Stargazing Tips & Equipment

Some of the best places to stargaze in Southern California include Joshua Tree National Park and Anza-Borrego Desert State Park, both far enough from city lights to reveal a much darker sky.

Aim for a night with little to no moon, since a bright moon washes out fainter stars and meteors. Give your eyes about 20-30 minutes to fully adjust to the dark before expecting to see much. Weeknights right after sunset work for bright constellations, but meteor showers are usually best after midnight.

You don't need a telescope to get started. Your eyes alone can pick out constellations, and a basic pair of binoculars will reveal star clusters and more detail in the Milky Way. Bring a red flashlight (it preserves night vision better than white light), a blanket or reclining chair, and a jacket to stay warm, since desert stargazing spots cool off fast after sunset.

Stellarium is a free planetarium app that shows exactly what's overhead in real time, just point your phone at the sky. It's a great way to confirm you are looking at the right constellation.
